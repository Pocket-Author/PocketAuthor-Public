# Provenance Will Become a Reader Experience

For a long time, provenance has lived backstage.

Editors care.

Researchers care.

Lawyers care.

Readers may see a citation or acknowledgments page, but most of the source trail remains invisible.

That may change.

As AI-generated and transformed content becomes more common, readers may increasingly want to know how a work was made.

## “Where did this come from?” becomes a product question

Imagine a digital book where a reader can inspect:

- source behind a claim
- original interview
- updated statistic
- translation note
- revision history
- image license
- author commentary

Not every reader will open those layers.

But knowing they exist can increase trust.

## Provenance can be selective

A reader experience should not overwhelm the page with metadata.

The surface remains readable.

The deeper trail becomes available when useful.

That could resemble expandable evidence.

The reader chooses depth.

## Updated editions become easier to understand

A book may change after publication.

New evidence.

Corrected statistic.

Revised chapter.

Updated policy.

A provenance-aware edition can explain what changed.

That is useful for readers who rely on the book professionally.

## AI transformation makes origin more important

If an audiobook uses synthetic narration, should the reader know?

If an illustration is AI-generated, should that be disclosed?

If a translation used machine assistance, how was it reviewed?

If an excerpt was adapted from another edition, what is the relationship?

These may become normal reader questions.

## Rights and provenance intersect

Readers may also care whether:

- interviews were consented
- images were licensed
- quotations are accurate
- Indigenous or community knowledge was used responsibly

Provenance can support ethical publishing.

## This does not mean radical transparency for everything

Some source material is private.

Some interviews are confidential.

Some editorial history should remain internal.

The goal is not to publish the entire workspace.

The goal is to preserve enough structured history that the author can make deliberate disclosure decisions.

## PocketAuthor can prepare for this future

- citations
- fact checking
- permissions
- reader notes
- updated editions
- disclosure
