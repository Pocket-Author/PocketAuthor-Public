# Keep Human Approval Visible

AI can make book production faster.

It can summarize.

Suggest.

Compare.

Rewrite.

Organize.

Draft.

Classify.

Generate alternatives.

But speed creates a governance question:

**Which decisions has the author actually approved?**

That should never become ambiguous.

## Suggestions are not decisions

A system can suggest:

- a new title
- a chapter rewrite
- a stronger claim
- a different structure
- a market category
- a price
- a publisher
- a quotation

But a suggestion becoming part of the book is a meaningful state change.

That change should remain visible.

## Some decisions deserve explicit approval

Especially:

- thesis
- major structural edits
- sensitive stories
- factual claims
- quotations
- permissions
- title/subtitle
- cover
- metadata
- rights
- pricing
- publication
- distribution

The higher the consequence, the clearer the approval should be.

## Human approval protects voice

AI can make prose smoother while making it less specific.

It can normalize unusual language.

It can remove tension.

It can make every chapter sound equally polished and equally generic.

Human review protects the patterns that make the author recognizable.

## Human approval protects accountability

When a book makes a factual claim, someone is responsible for publishing it.

When the book names a person, someone is responsible.

When it uses a copyrighted image, someone is responsible.

AI cannot hold that responsibility.

The author and publishing team can.

## Approval history can become editorial memory

Imagine being able to see:

- what changed
- who proposed it
- who approved it
- when
- why

That is useful in collaboration.

It is especially useful in long projects where people forget the reasoning behind older decisions.

## The product should not hide automation

Trust improves when the system is clear about:

- generated suggestions
- source material
- verified claims
- author-approved content
- unresolved issues

The goal is not to prove that no AI was involved.

The goal is to preserve accountable authorship.

## PocketAuthor principle

**Automate the work around judgment without hiding judgment itself.**

That is a much better responsible-AI position than simply saying “human in the loop.”
