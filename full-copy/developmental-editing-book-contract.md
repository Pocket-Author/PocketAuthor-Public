# Developmental Editing Begins With the Book’s Contract

You cannot judge the structure of a book until you know what the book has promised.

A chapter may be beautifully written and still be wrong for the project.

A section may be fascinating and still distract the reader.

A story may be powerful and still belong in another book.

Developmental editing begins with the book’s contract.

## The contract

The contract is a simple set of decisions:

- **Reader:** who is this for?
- **Problem:** what are they facing?
- **Promise:** what should become clearer or possible?
- **Scope:** what does this book cover?
- **Boundary:** what does it deliberately not cover?
- **Author advantage:** why is this author positioned to write it?

Everything structural can be evaluated against those decisions.

## Structure is not abstract

People sometimes say:

“The structure feels off.”

That is difficult to act on.

A stronger developmental note says:

“Chapter three introduces implementation tactics before the reader understands the decision framework promised in chapter two.”

Now the problem is explicit.

## Every chapter needs a reason to exist

For each chapter, ask:

- Which part of the reader promise does this chapter deliver?
- What does the reader know afterward?
- Why must it appear here?
- What evidence supports it?
- What happens if we remove it?

If the answer is weak, the chapter may be doing the wrong job.

## Developmental editing can add material

Good developmental editing is not only cutting.

Sometimes the book needs:

- a missing chapter
- a new example
- additional research
- a clearer definition
- a counterargument
- a better ending

The correct edit is whatever makes the contract easier to fulfill.

## Scope creep is a contract problem

Authors often add chapters because the new topic is interesting.

The contract helps decide whether it belongs.

Does this topic help the reader receive the promised transformation?

If not, save it for another article, course, appendix, or book.

## The contract should survive marketing

A strong contract helps the author write:

- title
- subtitle
- description
- proposal
- landing page
- launch messaging

That is because the editorial promise and marketing promise should not contradict each other.
