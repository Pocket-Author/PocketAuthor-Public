# Choose Distribution Backward From the Reader

Authors often begin distribution planning with a platform.

“Should I use Amazon?”

“Should I use Ingram?”

“Should I sell direct?”

Those are valid questions.

But they are not the first question.

The first question is:

**Where should the reader be able to get this book?**

## Start with reader behavior

Different readers buy books differently.

A general consumer may expect Amazon.

A professional association may buy in bulk.

A university may purchase through institutional systems.

An independent bookstore needs wholesale availability and workable economics.

An event audience may buy directly from the author.

An international reader may need local availability.

Distribution should follow those realities.

## One book may need multiple routes

A professional author might combine:

- Amazon availability
- wholesale distribution
- direct sales
- bulk/corporate sales
- event inventory
- audiobook channels

That is not redundant if each route serves a different reader context.

## Availability is not demand

Making the book orderable is only one layer.

The store still needs a reason to stock it.

The reader still needs a reason to buy it.

Distribution solves access.

Marketing solves demand.

Do not confuse them.

## Each route creates obligations

Different channels may create different:

- discounts
- fees
- returns
- print costs
- shipping
- metadata requirements
- account setup
- reporting

The author should understand those tradeoffs.
