# A Better Book Team Starts With a Better Brief

Authors often hire the right person with the wrong instructions.

“Edit my book.”

“Help with marketing.”

“Make the cover.”

“Do the research.”

Those requests describe a profession.

They do not describe a project.

A better book team starts with a better brief.

## Every role needs a finish line

A developmental editor and a copy editor are both editors.

They are not responsible for the same outcome.

A publicist and a marketer both help the book reach people.

They do not use the same methods.

A researcher and fact checker both work with evidence.

Their tasks differ.

The brief should make the distinction explicit.

## Define the problem first

Before hiring, write:

What is wrong or incomplete right now?

Examples:

- the manuscript structure is unclear
- citations are inconsistent
- the cover does not signal the genre
- we need interviews for missing evidence
- the launch has no podcast outreach plan

Now the work can be scoped.

## Define inputs

What will the professional receive?

- manuscript
- source library
- style sheet
- project brief
- interview transcripts
- brand assets
- target reader
- comparable titles

Missing inputs create delays.

## Define deliverables

What exactly should come back?

Examples:

- editorial letter
- chapter-by-chapter notes
- corrected manuscript
- source audit
- cover concepts
- production files
- media list
- outreach copy

A role title is not a deliverable.

## Define authority

Can the collaborator:

- suggest?
- edit directly?
- approve?
- publish?
- contact third parties?
- access private source material?

Permissions should be clear before the work starts.

## Define review rounds

How many rounds?

Who approves?

What counts as new scope?

When is the work complete?

These questions protect both author and professional.

## A brief also makes pricing easier

Professionals can price better when the scope is legible.

Authors can compare proposals more fairly.

Vague briefs create hidden work.

Hidden work creates frustration.
