# Protect the Author’s Voice During Collaboration

Books are collaborative more often than they appear.

Editors.

Ghostwriters.

Researchers.

Fact checkers.

Designers.

AI assistants.

Each person can improve the manuscript.

Each person can also accidentally make the author disappear.

## Cleaner is not always better

A sentence may become grammatically cleaner and emotionally flatter.

A paragraph may become more professional and less specific.

A story may become shorter and lose the detail that made it believable.

The goal is not to preserve every original sentence.

The goal is to preserve the author's characteristic judgment.

## Define what collaborators are allowed to change

Different collaborators should have different permissions.

A copy editor may correct grammar and consistency.

A developmental editor may challenge structure.

A ghostwriter may draft substantial prose.

A researcher may flag claims.

An AI assistant may propose alternatives.

Those are different authorities.

Making that visible prevents confusion.

## Require a reason for consequential edits

For major changes, the collaborator should be able to explain the purpose.

Is the change for:

- clarity
- accuracy
- structure
- consistency
- legal risk
- house style
- accessibility

That makes review easier.

## Build a voice reference

The author can provide:

- approved writing samples
- phrases they use
- phrases they dislike
- preferred level of formality
- how they handle uncertainty
- how they use humor
- preferred evidence density
- how directly they address the reader

This is especially useful for ghostwriting and AI assistance.

## Protect unusual judgment

Some of the most valuable writing sounds unusual because the author notices something differently.

Collaboration can accidentally normalize that.

Before accepting a smoother rewrite, ask:

Did we make this easier to read?

Or did we make it easier to ignore?

## Comments need decisions

Collaborative manuscripts can become clogged with unresolved discussion.

Every significant comment should eventually become:

- accept
- reject
- revise
- research
- ask author
- defer

Discussion is not the finish line.

Decision is.

### Practical exercise

Create a collaboration brief before the next editing round.

State:

- what the collaborator should change
- what they should flag
- what they should never alter without approval

That one page can prevent weeks of confusion.
