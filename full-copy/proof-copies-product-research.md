# Proof Copies Are Product Research

A proof copy is not a ceremonial object you order so you can take a photo holding your almost-finished book.

It is a test build.

Treat it like one.

## The screen hides physical problems

A print PDF can look perfect on a laptop while producing a disappointing book.

The physical object introduces:

- paper
- ink
- binding
- trim
- gutter
- spine
- cover finish
- manufacturing variation

You need to see the actual object.

## Review like a reader

Do not only check typos.

Hold it.

Open it.

Flip through quickly.

Read several pages.

Ask:

- Is the text too dense?
- Is the gutter comfortable?
- Are headings clear?
- Are images readable?
- Does the cover feel balanced?
- Is the spine legible?
- Do page breaks create awkward empty space?
- Are running heads distracting?
- Does the book feel like the category it belongs to?

## Review like a manufacturer

Also inspect:

- bleed
- margins
- image quality
- barcode area
- spine alignment
- crop
- page order
- blank pages
- front matter
- metadata

## Keep a proof checklist

Memory is unreliable.

Use the same review categories every time.

That makes proof rounds comparable.

## Order another proof after meaningful changes

If you change layout, paper, cover, trim, or major pagination, the previous proof may no longer represent the final object.

Do not assume.

## Proofing is not failure

Finding issues in a proof means the process worked.

The purpose is to discover problems before readers do.
