# Publicity and Marketing Are Related but Different

Authors often use “marketing” as a catch-all word for everything that happens after the book is finished.

That makes planning harder.

Publicity and marketing overlap.

They are not the same.

## Publicity seeks earned attention

Publicity may include:

- media coverage
- reviews
- interviews
- podcast appearances
- news angles
- features
- event coverage

The author or publicist pitches.

An independent gatekeeper decides whether the story is worth covering.

That means outcomes are not fully controllable.

## Marketing creates repeatable paths to discovery

Marketing may include:

- email
- social content
- paid advertising
- landing pages
- partnerships
- events
- direct outreach
- reader communities

The author has more control over the channel and message.

## Publicity is often story-driven

A journalist needs a reason the book matters now.

That might be:

- current event
- new research
- unusual author story
- cultural debate
- timely issue

“New book available” is rarely enough.

## Marketing can be evergreen

A useful guide.

A strong search page.

A newsletter.

A workshop.

A podcast archive.

These can continue producing discovery long after launch week.

## They should support each other

A media appearance can drive people to:

- book page
- newsletter
- event
- retailer
- direct store

Marketing infrastructure turns temporary attention into a longer reader relationship.

## Measure differently

Publicity may track:

- coverage
- interviews
- review quality
- referral traffic

Marketing may track:

- subscriber growth
- conversion
- registrations
- sales
- qualified leads

Do not evaluate every channel with the same metric.

## PocketAuthor can separate the workflows

- publicity targets

- success event
