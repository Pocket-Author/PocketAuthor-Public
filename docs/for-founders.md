# PocketAuthor for Founders

## Your company history may already contain a book

A founder's archive is often an unwritten book.

Years of product decisions, customer conversations, failed experiments, internal memos, fundraising narratives, talks, research, hiring lessons, and operating principles can contain a body of knowledge worth preserving.

The company story itself is not automatically the book. The **hard-earned framework inside the story** might be.

## Good founder-book angles

- a category thesis the market still misunderstands
- the operating system behind a company
- lessons learned building in a difficult industry
- a technical or regulatory field guide
- a founder philosophy
- a practical playbook for the next generation
- lessons from failure
- a history of an emerging market
- a manifesto for how an industry should change

## From company knowledge to book

1. Identify the recurring problem across your work.
2. Define the one reader who most needs the lesson.
3. Gather approved source material: notes, decks, talks, memos, interviews, research, and public artifacts.
4. Find the framework hiding inside the chronology.
5. Build a source-grounded outline.
6. Draft without flattening the founder's voice.
7. Verify every factual claim and sensitive story.
8. Separate public lessons from confidential information.
9. Prepare the manuscript for production.
10. Turn the finished book into an authority, teaching, speaking, and business asset.

## The book is bigger than the manuscript

A founder book can support:

- category creation
- thought leadership
- recruiting
- customer education
- partner trust
- speaking
- workshops
- fundraising credibility
- community building
- legacy

PocketAuthor is designed to keep the manuscript, sources, publishing workflow, marketing, rights, and next actions connected so the founder can focus on the work only they can do.
