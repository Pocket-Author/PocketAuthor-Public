# Authoring and Publishing Glossary

**Manuscript** — the working text of a book before final publication.

**Outline** — the planned structure of a book, usually organized into parts, chapters, and sections.

**Developmental editing** — editing focused on structure, argument, organization, pacing, and overall effectiveness.

**Line editing** — sentence- and paragraph-level editing for clarity, style, flow, and voice.

**Copy editing** — editing for grammar, consistency, usage, factual details, and style conventions.

**Proofreading** — final review for errors after the manuscript is close to publication.

**Beta reader** — an early reader who gives feedback before publication.

**ARC / advance reader copy** — a pre-publication version distributed to reviewers or early readers.

**ISBN** — an International Standard Book Number used to identify a specific book edition.

**Copyright** — legal protection for qualifying original works of authorship.

**Public domain** — works not protected by copyright in a particular jurisdiction or context.

**Fair use / fair dealing** — legal doctrines that may permit certain uses of copyrighted material depending on jurisdiction and circumstances.

**Citation** — a reference identifying the source of information, evidence, or a quotation.

**Attribution** — credit given to the person or source associated with material or ideas.

**Source provenance** — information about where content, evidence, quotations, or claims originated.

**Ghostwriting** — writing produced for another person who is presented as the author or principal author.

**Co-authoring** — writing a work collaboratively with multiple credited authors.

**Self-publishing** — publishing a book without a traditional publisher controlling the publication process.

**Traditional publishing** — publication through a publishing house that acquires rights and manages production and distribution.

**Hybrid publishing** — publishing models combining features of traditional and author-funded publishing.

**Metadata** — structured information describing a book, such as title, author, categories, description, keywords, and identifiers.

**Trim size** — the physical dimensions of a printed book.

**Ebook** — a digital book edition.

**Audiobook** — an audio edition of a book.

**Distribution** — the systems and channels through which books become available to retailers, libraries, platforms, and readers.

**Author platform** — the audience, reputation, channels, relationships, and visibility an author can use to reach readers.

**Query letter** — a pitch sent to a literary agent or publisher.

**Literary agent** — a representative who may help authors sell publishing rights and negotiate contracts.

**Translation rights** — rights to translate a work into another language.

**Adaptation rights** — rights to adapt a work into another medium, such as film or television.
