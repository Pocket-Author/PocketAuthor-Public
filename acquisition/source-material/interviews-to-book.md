# How to Turn Interviews Into a Book

PocketAuthor's core source-material idea is simple:

**you may already have more of the book than you think.**

The challenge is turning material created for another purpose into a coherent reader journey.

## What you may already have

- recorded interviews
- transcripts
- research notes
- speaker bios
- follow-up questions

## A practical workflow

1. Define the question the whole book is trying to answer.
2. Tag interviews by theme.
3. Separate interviewee claims from verified facts.
4. Choose whether the author or interviewees carry the narrative voice.
5. Group complementary and conflicting perspectives.
6. Build chapters around questions or themes.
7. Request clarification where transcripts are ambiguous.
8. Confirm quotation accuracy.
9. Track consent and permissions.
10. Add synthesis so the book becomes more than a transcript collection.

## Common mistake

**A book of interviews still needs editorial synthesis and a point of view.**

The new book needs its own promise, structure, evidence, voice, and editorial logic.

## Questions to ask before drafting

- Who is this book for?
- What should the reader understand or do afterward?
- What material is uniquely yours?
- What material needs permission?
- Which claims need updated evidence?
- Which parts repeat each other?
- What is missing from the archive?
- Which content was created for a different audience or platform?
- What should be left out?
