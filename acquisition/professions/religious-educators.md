# Book Ideas for Religious Educators

## What should religious educators write a book about?

A strong professional book turns repeated experience into a useful framework for a specific reader.

## Five directions to test

1. **How to Teach Tradition in a Modern Context** — validate it against your real experience, evidence, and audience.
2. **A Field Guide to Foundational Questions** — validate it against your real experience, evidence, and audience.
3. **What Learners Repeatedly Misunderstand** — validate it against your real experience, evidence, and audience.
4. **A Structured Guide to Practice and Meaning** — validate it against your real experience, evidence, and audience.
5. **How to Teach With Sources and Humility** — validate it against your real experience, evidence, and audience.

## Source material you may already have

- lesson notes
- public lectures
- primary religious texts
- commentary
- student questions

## Questions that reveal the strongest direction

- What do outsiders misunderstand?
- What do beginners repeatedly get wrong?
- What problem have you solved many times?
- What changed your mind?
- Which lesson took years to learn?
- What can you support with evidence?
- Which stories illustrate the lesson?
- Who needs this knowledge most?
- What should change after reading?

## What to protect

Handle sacred texts, attribution, denominational differences, and pastoral or student confidentiality with care.
