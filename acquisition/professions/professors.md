# Book Ideas for Professors

## What should professors write a book about?

Professional experience becomes a strong book when it is narrowed into one useful promise for one reader.

The best starting point is not “everything I know.” It is the combination of repeated problems, distinctive judgment, source material, and evidence that gives the author an advantage.

## The strongest raw material

- lectures
- syllabi
- papers
- seminars
- student questions

Ask which materials contain original decisions, repeated patterns, or explanations you have refined over time.

## Questions that reveal the book

- What do outsiders misunderstand?
- Which decision requires more judgment than it appears?
- What do smart newcomers repeatedly get wrong?
- What took you years to learn?
- What changed your mind?
- Which problem have you solved many times?
- What evidence can you show?
- Which stories are safe and ethical to publish?
- Who most needs this?
- What should the reader do differently afterward?

## A useful book structure

1. The misunderstood problem
2. The context readers need
3. The author’s core framework
4. The decisions that matter
5. Common failure modes
6. Examples and counterexamples
7. A practical operating model
8. What changes next

## What to protect

Protect student privacy, respect coauthor rights, and distinguish published evidence from unpublished work.

Professional authority grows when the book is precise about what is known, what is opinion, what is confidential, and what may change.
