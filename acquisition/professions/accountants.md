# Book Ideas for Accountants

## What should accountants write a book about?

A strong professional book begins with **author advantage**: repeated experience, distinctive judgment, evidence, and source material that readers cannot easily get elsewhere.

## Five directions to test

1. **What Small Businesses Misunderstand About Their Numbers** — validate it against the author’s real experience, audience, and evidence.
2. **How Accountants Read Risk in Financial Statements** — validate it against the author’s real experience, audience, and evidence.
3. **A Field Guide to Better Financial Controls** — validate it against the author’s real experience, audience, and evidence.
4. **The Questions Owners Should Ask Before Tax Season** — validate it against the author’s real experience, audience, and evidence.
5. **How to Build Financial Discipline Inside a Growing Company** — validate it against the author’s real experience, audience, and evidence.

## Source material already hiding in the work

- client education
- public tax guidance
- training materials
- financial-control checklists
- seminars

## Questions that reveal the book

- What do outsiders misunderstand?
- What took years to learn?
- What do people repeatedly ask you to explain?
- Which mistakes keep recurring?
- What changed your mind?
- What can you support with evidence?
- Who needs this knowledge most?
- What should change after the reader finishes?

## What to protect

Avoid client-specific financial information and keep tax/accounting rules jurisdiction- and date-specific.
