# Book Ideas for Software Engineers

## What should a software engineer write a book about?

The best professional books usually come from **author advantage**: experience, evidence, judgment, repeated questions, and source material that outsiders cannot easily reproduce.

For software engineers, the strongest starting point is rarely “write everything you know.” It is usually one recurring problem, one reader, and one useful transformation.

## Five book directions to test

1. **The Architecture Decisions That Age Well** — validate this against your real experience, evidence, and audience.
2. **What Junior Engineers Learn Too Late** — validate this against your real experience, evidence, and audience.
3. **How to Debug Systems, Not Symptoms** — validate this against your real experience, evidence, and audience.
4. **A Practical Guide to Technical Tradeoffs** — validate this against your real experience, evidence, and audience.
5. **Engineering Lessons From Production Failures** — validate this against your real experience, evidence, and audience.

These are starting hypotheses, not prescriptions. The strongest direction should be grounded in work you actually did and material you can responsibly use.

## Find the book hidden inside the job

Ask yourself:

- What do outsiders repeatedly misunderstand?
- What do smart newcomers get wrong?
- What problem have you solved many times?
- What changed your mind?
- What do people keep asking you to explain?
- What material already documents your thinking?
- What can you support with evidence?
- Who specifically needs this book?
- What should the reader be able to do differently afterward?

## Source material you may already have

- design docs
- postmortems
- technical talks
- code review notes
- architecture diagrams

PocketAuthor's larger thesis is that the book can begin with this body of work instead of a blank page.

## A useful chapter architecture

1. The problem readers misread
2. What experience changed your perspective
3. The core framework
4. Common mistakes
5. How professionals actually decide
6. Examples and counterexamples
7. A practical playbook
8. What changes next

## What to protect

Remove credentials, secrets, proprietary code, and confidential company details.

A professional book becomes stronger when permissions, privacy, sources, and uncertainty are handled explicitly rather than cleaned up at the end.
