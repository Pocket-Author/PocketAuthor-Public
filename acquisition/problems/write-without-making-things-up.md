# How Do I Write a Book Without Making Things Up?

Build a claim-review habit into the manuscript from the beginning.

## The short answer

Separate memory, opinion, inference, and verified fact; attach sources to important claims and mark uncertainty instead of smoothing it away.

## Diagnose the real problem

Ask:

- Is the reader clear?
- Is the book promise clear?
- Is the scope too broad?
- Is the outline doing too many jobs?
- Is the source material organized?
- Are there unresolved factual or permission questions?
- Is the next step concrete?
- Are you trying to solve editing, research, and production at the same time?

## A practical reset

1. Write the reader in one sentence.
2. Write the promise in one sentence.
3. List the chapters that are absolutely necessary.
4. Remove chapters that do not serve the promise.
5. List the strongest source material for each chapter.
6. Mark unanswered research questions.
7. Define the next review stage.
8. Work on one stage at a time.
