# PocketAuthor for Founders

## Turn company-building experience into a book that outlasts the company timeline

Founders accumulate valuable knowledge in fragments:

- pitch decks
- internal memos
- customer interviews
- product decisions
- hiring lessons
- market research
- fundraising narratives
- launch retrospectives
- talks and podcasts
- mistakes that changed the company

That material can become a book—but only if you find the durable idea inside the chronology.

## The strongest founder books are usually not “my startup story”

A useful founder book may be:

- a category thesis
- an operating playbook
- a market history
- a technical field guide
- a leadership framework
- a book about repeated failure modes
- a manifesto for how an industry should change

## A practical path

1. Gather the source material.
2. Identify the recurring lesson.
3. Define the reader.
4. Separate confidential material from public lessons.
5. Build the book thesis.
6. Create a chapter architecture.
7. Add evidence and interviews.
8. Draft in the founder’s real voice.
9. Fact-check claims.
10. Turn the book into speaking, recruiting, community and category assets.
