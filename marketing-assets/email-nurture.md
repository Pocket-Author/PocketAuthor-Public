# PocketAuthor Email Nurture Sequences

## Sequence A — From Body of Work to Book

### Email 1 — You may already have a book

**Subject:** Your book may already exist in pieces

You may not need more ideas.

You may need a better way to see the work you have already done.

Your notes, talks, client work, research, interviews, presentations, podcast appearances, frameworks, and years of hard-earned judgment may already contain the raw material for a book.

The problem is not always writing from scratch.

The problem is recognizing what belongs together.

PocketAuthor is built around that idea:

**from body of work to book.**

Start by gathering a few representative pieces of your existing work.

Then ask:

What question am I repeatedly trying to help people answer?

That question may be more important than the files themselves.

### Email 2 — The blank page is often the wrong place to start

**Subject:** Stop starting from a blank page

A blank page can be useful.

But it is not always the best starting point for a professional book.

If you already have years of material, the stronger workflow is:

**source material → recurring ideas → reader → promise → outline**

before you ask for more words.

Bring in the work you already have.

Then decide what deserves to become a book.

**CTA:** Start with your source material.

### Email 3 — A topic is not a book

**Subject:** “Leadership” is not a book yet

A topic names territory.

A book needs a promise.

Who is the reader?

What are they trying to understand or do?

What changes after reading?

What makes your perspective useful?

Once those questions are clear, the outline becomes much easier.

### Email 4 — AI should not erase the author

**Subject:** AI can help. It should not replace authorship.

AI can accelerate:

- organization
- synthesis
- drafting
- editing
- comparison

But it should not silently replace:

- judgment
- evidence
- permissions
- voice
- author approval

PocketAuthor is designed around a simple principle:

**AI should make the work around authorship easier without making authorship less meaningful.**

### Email 5 — Your draft is not yet a published book

**Subject:** Finished draft ≠ publish-ready book

A manuscript may still need:

- permissions
- copy editing
- proofing
- metadata
- ISBN decisions
- cover
- EPUB
- print files
- proof copies
- distribution

Publishing is a chain of decisions.

Not an upload button.
